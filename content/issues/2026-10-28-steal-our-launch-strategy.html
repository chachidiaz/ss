We just had our most successful launch of the year and here’s how we did it.

First, the context

2025 was a beautiful year personally and professionally. Our daughter was born alongside Emily’s signature course, the AAC Assessment Hub. Our first launch generated over $50,000 in revenue and more than paid for maternity leave. The second launch generated around $23,000 in revenue just six months later.

We knew in October 2025 that we wouldn’t re-launch until a year later and here’s why.

Launch Ingredients

Live launching needs a few things to be successful:

  1. A really good, no-brainer take-my-money type of offer
  2. A dialed-in email sequence and social content
  3. Traffic and audience

The decision to launch AAC Hub a year later allowed us to rebuild number 3 and give us time to re-evaluate number 1.

Our second launch felt like it didn’t meet our goals but with a whole other ass human we were raising, we just needed to push pause and we needed time.

The Live Launch Strategy

Live launching is essentially a three step framework: evaluate based on available data, decide your launch mechanism, and execute the vision.

Step 1: The Evaluation

When I looked at this launch to determine how I wanted to go about positioning this specific sale, I went back to look at a few things.

  • Past email and social content performance: did the past content strategy drive traffic to our sales page and generate the interest and eyeballs we needed?
    • Instagram engagement rates/views
    • ManyChat runs and click-through rates
    • Email open rates/click-through rates
  • Sales page views: how many unique visitors did we have on the sales page and where did they come from? Did we get enough people seeing the offer in front of them?
  • Purchase conversion rates: based on how many people saw the sales page, how many actually purchased?

If we just took the revenue numbers at face value, that doesn’t tell me where we went wrong and how to make it better. Here is how I diagnose issues:

  • High sales page views but low purchase rate means the offer is misaligned to the audience or price is too high relative to the value of the offer.
  • Low sales page views, high purchase rate means offer was great but we didn’t get enough people to view the sales page.
  • High open rates (emails) and low click-through rates means the emails didn’t resonate and the messaging was off.
  • Low open rates and low click-through rates means your audience is misaligned and are tuning out (or your emails are going to spam)
  • Low open rates and high click-through rates means your subject lines need work.

This is a simplified version of the process I go through but generally, this is what I am evaluating.

Based on the data from our October 2025 launch, I felt like we got the views, email opens were good, click-through rates were good, but purchase conversion rate was lower than what I wanted so I started there.

I looked at the offer:

  1. Was the offer truly worth $187? Did we provide enough value inside the course to justify the higher price-tag for SLPs? We felt it was. So next is…
  2. Did the sales page truly communicate that offer effectively and answer objections, demonstrate what’s inside the course, and leave SLPs feeling like this was a no-brainer? This is where it fell short.

I knew we needed a new design and to beef up the parts of the page that spoke to the value of the offer itself.

We also had data that suggested the $187 price-point was too high so we:

  1. Lowered the price back to $147
  2. Majorly beefed up the what’s included section of the sales page
  3. Re-designed and added modern motion components for a branded, professional appeal
  4. Added the live cohort + virtual AAC course as bonuses to make it a no-brainer.

Great. Now that’s done, the question becomes: how do we want to launch this?

Step 2: Decide on a launch mechanism

I love live webinar/masterclasses. They generate energy and novelty, foster trust and value, and position the creator as an authority. It also perfectly prepares the consumer for purchase.

We knew from last launch the live webinar generated tons of interest (over 2,000 people) but less than 5% of people who signed up actually purchased.

If we were going to repeat that process, we needed to have a topic that organically opened up a knowledge gap between the free content and the paid AAC Hub.

That’s where the AAC Evals for Beginners came from. It was a topic Emily could deliver on her promise of providing real value (not just a sales pitch) and still have people wanting more.

Over 2,000 registered, but fewer than 300 showed up, but this was intentional.

I go back and forth on offering show-up bonuses to encourage live participation. With a large majority of our audience being parents, moms specifically, we don’t want to pressure them to show up live if they can’t. It’s important that the people who show up actually consume the content, are focused, and receptive vs distracted, multi-tasking, and anxiously awaiting the free bonus they were promised.

28 people purchased during the live masterclass! That’s a great number so we might test a show-up bonus again to encourage live participation.

Step 3: Execute the vision

I outlined when we would send emails and the specific topic we want to cover and Emily writes the emails.

For this promotion, Emily created a new AAC related lead magnet and promoted it three weeks before the launch (reached an additional 1,000 email subscribers) and promoted the webinar roughly 10 days ahead of time (2,000 email subscribers).

This culminated in a roughly three week pre-launch runway of a list full of people who consumed free valuable content, positioned to buy, and targeted specifically for their interest in AAC evaluation.

We sent emails to promote the masterclass + to promote the sale.

Day 1 Tuesday: Cart open announcement

Day 2 Wednesday: Resend to unopens only

Day 3 Thursday: Cohort bonus explanation

Day 4 Friday: Surprise bonus announcement

Day 5 Saturday: No email

Day 6 Sunday: Soft urgency (closes Tuesday)

Day 7 Monday: Soft urgency (FAQs and objection handling)

Day 8 AM: Hard urgency (offer recap)

Day 8 PM: Hard urgency reminder

In addition, Emily strategically uses her Instagram stories to stay top of mind daily to her audience and plans 3-4 in-feed posts of conversion content meant to specifically speak to the pain points of the buyer and the transformation.

Our audience should see the offer anywhere from 3-10 times within the promotional offer window.

The Early Results

This launch JUST ended so we haven’t done a full launch debrief but early results are positive.

Our October 2025 launch was for a $187 offer, for 12 days (Monday through the following Friday). That’s a long time and a lot of emails. We made roughly $23k.

For this launch, the offer was $147 for 8 days and we’ve made $26k.

This tells me there’s a higher conversion rate so our hard work paid off. If we extended the sale for the full 12 days (there will be plenty of people who will ask to come in) then I imagine we would hit $30k fairly easily.

So why don’t we? Well, we kind of will.

For our full list, the promotion is done. But we’ll email smaller segments of people to extend their deadline to encourage them to purchase. We’ll let in the SLPs who email in and get pretty close to that $30k mark.

All in all, it’s a win for us! Another $50k launch would be fantastic, but there are so many reasons why it’s hard to replicate those results. But, for now, The Hub won’t be launched again for another year and we have it on evergreen where it generates consistent revenue every single day.
